HPLC analysis — astaxanthin via apo-carotenal IS

Calibrate on the area ratio → matrix-spike diagnostic → astaxanthin recovered as an amount (µg), blind to recovery and reconstitution volume, with inverse-prediction 95% intervals.

Read once. Three things decide whether the number is right:
  1. Spike the IS at the very start, before any extraction — into samples and matrix spikes. That is the only reason recovery and final volume cancel.
  2. Quantify on the ratio (done for you). The AUC columns are inputs, not the answer — never read raw astaxanthin area off the curve.
  3. Set the two anchors below. The standards' IS is a concentration; the sample spike is an absolute amount. They are used on opposite sides of the maths and need not match — a mismatch washes out.

What the interval means. The 95% interval is the inverse-prediction interval from the calibration fit — it reflects how well the line is determined and where the sample falls on it, not replicate injection scatter (enter that separately if you have it). A sample whose ratio falls outside the calibrated range is extrapolation and is flagged. When correction is applied, the interval is an approximate combination of calibration and correction-factor uncertainty.
